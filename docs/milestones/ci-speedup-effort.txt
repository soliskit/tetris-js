Faster CI

A measured plan for pull-request feedback and deployment speed.

Charter | Project: soliskit/tetris-js

## Goal

Faster CI means less time waiting for reliable evidence, not less evidence.
This program finds the fastest safe configuration for pull-request feedback and
main-to-deployment latency, measured and reported separately. There is no
arbitrary hard latency target.

Measure the final tree first. Keep every remaining protection. Change only the
CI mechanism under test. Retain an optimization only when the complete
configuration shows a material gain without worse reliability.

## Current status

## Baseline figures

Historical Phase-0 figures, approximate, from the baseline record (kept in git
history):

- test job: 2 min 30 s
- browser install: 49 s combined for Chromium and WebKit
- browser tests plus coverage: 79 s on 2 workers
- typecheck: about 1 s
- unit tests: about 2 s
- setup/install: about 14 s per ordinary job
- full mutation job: about 32 min 39 s; the Stryker step inside it: about
  32 min 17 s (two different measures, not conflicting baselines)
- 1,152 mutants
- identical-code timeout totals observed: 24, 31 and 32

These are historical comparison figures, not the future control and not a
future mutant universe. One open verification: QA-1 enforcement was located in
the review's pinned snapshot at test/traceability.test.js; that is a lead, not
activation-time verification, and its checks must keep meaningful protection
under any new topology.

## Protections

The QA-1 through QA-6 requirements remain unchanged. This program does not
authorize test removal, lower unit/engine coverage, lower browser page-script
coverage, a lower mutation threshold or narrower required mutation scope, fewer
required browser or device projects, retries used to turn failures into
acceptance, weaker required-check semantics, or expanding the trusted core to
reduce CI cost. Changing a required-check name, a branch-protection setting,
scope, cost, or any requirement or certification policy stays the owner's
decision; so does merging, releasing or deploying. A mechanism is kept only
when its measured gain clears the control's own spread and at least ten
percent of the control median, every protection is unchanged, and reliability
does not get worse; a candidate that does not clear the rule is dropped without
escalation. The detailed benchmark methodology (measurement controls, samples,
negative controls and acceptance rules) is recorded in the pre-rewrite charter,
kept in git history.
