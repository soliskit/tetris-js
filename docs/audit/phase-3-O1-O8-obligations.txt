This record was rewritten in place on October 25, 2026 from the longer candidate paper of the same name, which is in git history (the last commit containing it is the parent of the rewriting commit; decision row D87 names it with its SHA-256). It keeps each property's assumptions and limits, the O6 trust ledger, the paper's nine mapping gaps (M1 to M9) and its other recorded unknowns. Each property's definition (the blueprint holds O1 to O8), proof method, covered-code mapping and evidence text are in git history only. The paper was written in compressed shorthand; the text below is re-spaced and lightly reworded, with the same meaning intended, and the paper's own wording is kept where it is unclear.

Status. Every record read Candidate or Not Assessed when written. This is candidate-stage text, not shown to be the limits behind any certification word, and not a certification status or a new finding. Entries that have since moved on are marked inline. The paper ran no project tests, mutation run, browser or device check. It inspected the revision 4 model source and hashed all 12 source, note and self-test freeze entries (all matched). Reading the references. ### Properties O1 to O8: assumptions and limits as recorded

- O1 (authoritative transition boundary). Assumptions: under D22, a successful timer cancel never fires, and failed registration or cancel is audited; under D23, a failed cancel does not mutate state and storage is trusted on success only. Trust that a browser call succeeded does not authorize project commits. Limits: mapping gaps remain. Direct input, touch and focus commits (A11 to A14, entries in the legend of `docs/audit/phase-3-writer-frame-checkpoint-matrix.txt`) lie outside the checked manager boundary. The softDrop confirmation clear happens before the guard (as written then; the closure record's Entry-wrapper entry now records this). The constructor and the opening are treated separately. Some public methods are exposed without a guard. A candidate violation needs a reproduction linked to a requirement and is not automatically a defect. The fault predicate, the report and the resource projection are unresolved. Caller choices and Undefined results restrict how the model can be used.
- O2. Assumptions: only the D22 and D23 timer and storage assumptions and the D24 valid random source are assumed, and none of them establishes that the project's own transitions are legal. No numeric safe-integer or divisibility constraint is added to R1's whole, non-negative score. Limits: the production monitor checks score as a safe integer and a multiple of 100, and its coverage of paused-piece fit, held piece, bag, controls, reset count and lowest row differs from full R1, so implication cannot be assumed. Still needed: a semantic absence projection at game over, state and resource correspondence, and safe-terminal observables. The revision 4 valid_state function is itself not independently accepted, and an opening queue supplied at construction is not validated. Gaps about kicks, spawn and timing are retained.
- O3. Assumptions: under D23 a successful storage operation is trusted but returned contents never are, and there is no durability or tamper guarantee. D21's choices for failed reads are preserved, and a failed read is not treated as equivalent to invalid content. Limits: the exact old save-format syntax is not modeled, and there is no independently justified raw-byte or semantic adapter. The model's L1 and L2 defaults are not recovered history. The production omission of reset count and lowest row from saves, and the rejection of numeric scores, need separate reproduction. Cross-tab eligibility and the session-withdrawal projection are pending. Unknown input partitions and the accepted whole semantics are not exhausted.
- O4. Assumptions: under D23 successful operations are trusted and failures do not stop gameplay; D21's last-good, no-rollback and withdraw semantics apply. No disk durability and no rollback of a caller's side effects is assumed. Limits: a per-effect preservation applicability analysis and a trace matrix of actual throw, validation and cancel cases are still needed. The order of the high-score write cannot be presumed to be a saved-game rule. In the model, an externally observable write callback can fire before the Undefined result, so preflight choices and the reject trace are needed, and preservation must not be inferred. F20 ordering and consequence and the F23 mechanism were still candidates at that stage, not treated as defects without reproduction (October 5; the certification record now cites F20 under STA-4 and F23 under SAF-3 as confirmed defects).
- O5. Assumptions: only accurate, dated D24 workflow results are assumed. Testing, coverage and converter tools need explicit accounting as evidence controls. The source repository is not verification of the currently deployed bytes. Limits: no completed differential adapters, traces or proofs, and no fresh validation of deployed bytes. D40 says Not Assessed means no method yet, and this record does not adopt that redefinition: many Not Assessed records have proposed methods but unassessed claims. Tag counts, configuration and old passing metrics do not certify anything. Attribution when coverage is converted using URLs only, and the independence of implementation-derived fixtures, remain open.
- O6. Assumptions: exactly the O6 ledger below is assumed, with no inferred additional trust. Successful-call browser semantics are an assumption with exclusions, not proof that the game uses a feature correctly. No defense against a compromised host or browser is assumed, and no permanent storage, clock accuracy, random fairness or future CI guarantee. Limits: a complete audit from each dependency to its assumption is still needed, including uses of event listeners, pointer capture, focus and the current bootstrap API that W4 does not automatically cover. Test tools, shims, raw-format adapters and model caller choices need accounting. The D24 settings are dated October 4, and no current enforcement claim is made. Earlier unresolved clock wording in D27 and D28 was superseded by D29 and is not a new clock assumption.
- O7. Assumptions: no exception was adopted under D16. A successful cancel is trusted. A failed cancel can fire, but a stopped game cannot change (D23 U3). The project's own public routes are never assumed safe because they are reachable. Limits: no bypass tests and no proof covering every writer. Still needed: literal SAF-4 checkpoint coverage of every action and timer, the representation of the selected choice, project and native focus authority, safe stop of repeats, and the failed-cancel callback. A route without the boundary is a Candidate and is not automatically a defect without a violation of a governing requirement. This supplement authorizes no permanent tests and no consolidation.
- O8. Assumptions: statements by the author and reviewers about provenance are reports, not proof that no exposure occurred, and an exception requires the original owner approval together with a replacement argument. Caller Env, choice and harness behavior is trusted only after accounting. No oracle acceptance is assumed. Limits: no complete audit of the author's exposure, and no independently rerun readiness check, raw-format mapping or production differential. Revision 1 and 2 defects (the T3 typo, the opening, B12) are preserved. The revision 3 Undefined mutation defect was addressed in revision 4 but not erased. Revision 4 can call external storage before the Undefined result and cannot undo it, so preflight and a reject trace are needed. A successful softDrop counter is not timer phase or deadline, and blocked softDrop and other reissues are absent. Choices about kicks, spawn, release, opening, start and blur, failed reads, taps, the high score and dialogs are partial. Real timing, device, display and input mapping, legacy formats and the high-score parser need separate methods. Phase 3 stays open: the inputs were approved, but the model and results were not (October 5 statement; not re-checked at fold time).

### O6 trust ledger as recorded

The O6 ledger lists thirteen dependencies the audit assumes, each with where its trust comes from, the assumption, known failures and the proof boundary (what lies outside the audit and what lies inside it). It invents no resolved facts.

1. Timer scheduler (setting and clearing gravity, lock and repeat firings). Trust comes from D22 T3 and D23 U3 to U4, adopted as sources, not from a test of the scheduler. Assumption: a successful cancel prevents any later firing; there is no guarantee that registration or cancel succeeds. Known failures: a throw or failed cancel whose callback may fire but must not change the stopped game. Outside: the documented semantics of a successful cancel. Inside: the project's requests, the error stop, handling of a failed cancel, and every action and timer checkpoint. There is no real-deadline or throttling guarantee; only the S10 requested value is used.
2. localStorage (payload, flag and high-score interface). Trust: D23 U1 with D21 S4 to S9. Assumption: a successful operation has its documented semantics; contents are untrusted. Known failures: denied access, throws, quota, read failure, edits and loss. Outside: that the API succeeded. Inside: validation, reconstruction, the order of withdraw and save, session item 16 and failures. There is no permanence, crash-durability or tamper guarantee.
3. CacheStorage (version files and pins). Trust: D24 V1. Assumption: a successful cache call has its documented semantics. Known failures: errors, partial attempts, a foreign same-origin entry, malformed pins. Outside: browser operations. Inside: the project's own names, completeness, selection, cleanup, pinning and error logic. No namespace isolation is assumed.
4. Hosting (deployed bytes). Trust: D24 V1 and dated Phase 0 deployment evidence, not a new run. Assumption: Pages serves the deployed files. Excluded failures: unavailability and account compromise. Outside: provider behavior. Inside: deployed-source identity, the relative graph, and APP-2 and APP-6 logic. The actual current deployed bytes were not checked.
5. GitHub Actions and settings (workflow execution and report). Trust: D24 V2 facts of October 4 and the Phase 0 baseline. Assumption: execution and report are accurate for the recorded run. Known failures: a skipped, cancelled or failed run, and changed rules or workflows. Outside: the provider's report. Inside: the workflow arguments, the scope of coverage and campaigns, and the interpretation of claims. No current settings guarantee is inferred from files.
6. Random source (bag values). Trust: D24 V3. Assumption: valid values only. Known failures: unexpected values are separate evidence; no fairness, unpredictability or security claim is made. Outside: the validity of the source. Inside: the bag algorithm, the deal and how the source is used. Controlled random sources and test controls are accounted separately.
7. Canvas and P3 (2D pixel and context operations). Trust: the D27 W1 to W5 and D35 W4 list. Assumption: documented semantics only, and only on success. Excluded failures: missing or refused features, throws, false feature availability, a browser lying about success. Outside: platform success. Inside: context order, arguments, conversion, and render, retry and reporting. Real device evidence is not supplied by trust.
8. Animation frames and gamepad (callback scheduling, controller read, connect and disconnect). Trust: the same D27 and D35 lists. Assumption: success semantics. Known failures: missing features, throws, disconnects and read errors, and project retries and loop failures. Outside: the provider. Inside: poll mapping, authority effects, scheduling, recovery and reporting. Drawing and polling are not U4 timers.
9. Wake lock (screen request and sentinel). Trust: D27. Assumption: success semantics, not guaranteed availability. Known failures: unsupported, refused or revoked locks, a deferred grant, release errors. Outside: the provider. Inside: the wanted policy, a stale grant, and revoke, retry, release and error handling. It is best effort and not an R1 predicate.
10. Size, viewport, device pixel ratio, matchMedia, dialog, page visibility, key, blur and activeElement. Trust: the facilities enumerated in D27 and D35. Assumption: success semantics, only for the listed features. Known failures: throws, missing features, resize and focus interleavings. Outside: browser semantics. Inside: the project's own layout, arguments, the dialog's selected choice, native focus projection and authority. Unlisted facilities are not automatically trusted.
11. Service worker registration and fetch. Trust: D28 X1 and X2. Assumption: documented semantics on success, with no guarantee of success. Known failures: refusal, throws, network failure and handling of the returned response. Outside: the browser. Inside: the registration path, downloads, handling and version logic.
12. Clock. Trust: D29 and D35. Assumption: none; accuracy, steadiness and monotonic behavior are not assumed. Known failures: forward and backward jumps. The clock is inside the audit whenever cleanup or load decisions depend on it. It is not proof that deleting is safe.
13. Test controls and tools (the model Env, caller choices, random shims, storage and failure controls, Node, Playwright, coverage, Stryker, compiler and harness). Trust: audit controls F5 and F6 and D27 W5 stand-ins; tool configuration and exact version evidence are needed. Assumption: observability of controlled outcomes and reliable attribution are not blindly assumed. Known failures: instrumentation bias, expected outputs derived from the code, stale data, raw-byte or semantic adapter error, and Undefined side effects. Boundary: the evidence machinery is an explicit, reviewed boundary; it is no certification of a provider's implementation and no oracle is derived from production. No grant to trust unlisted tools or choices is added.

### Mapping gaps for the properties as recorded (open at that stage)

- M1. The eight property records fill a documentary omission but remain candidates. Every appropriate route, state, property and model mapping still needs independent evidence. Fifty-five tags and eight records cannot close Phase 3 by count.
- M2. For O1 and O7, the complete writer table and frame and checkpoint table is missing, especially A11 focus, A12 tokens, A13 repeat, A14 touch, the A16 withdraw, the pre-guard confirmation, the constructor and exposed methods. Each candidate must be linked to a governing requirement; a public method is not assumed to be a defect or an approved exception.
- M3. For O2, the full R1 semantic projection and the implication from the monitor are unavailable. Ordinary and fault predicates and resources must be kept separate. The revision 4 model has had only a bounded review, and the source valid_state is not automatically the true oracle.
- M4. For O3, adapters for semantic save, the raw released format and the high score, and independent malformed domains, are missing. For O4, evidence of per-effect preservation, cut points and the observed storage consequence is missing. An Undefined rollback cannot rewind a caller's write, so all choices must be preflighted or the trace discarded.
- M5. For O5, the ledger of exact current shipped bytes, code, independent test, proof and source attribution is not complete. Old Phase 0 metrics are not proof of a current claim. The model's method list is not an executed relation to production.
- M6. For O6, the dependency list must be reconciled against all actual call uses within the adopted limited scope. Success assumptions are not an availability guarantee or proof of the project's handling. Current settings and deployment were not verified. The clock is explicitly untrusted. Test controls, adapters, choices and unlisted facilities remain accounting gaps.
- M7. For O8, the authorship and exposure argument needs a verified provenance chain rather than self-reports by the author and reviewers. All four attachment URLs were recovered from the original event. Delivered-byte retrieval was blocked with HTTP 403, so exact byte equality remains unverified. All 12 freeze identities and four committed pack hashes were verified; the chronology that the freeze came before the comparison is reported, not independently proved. Changes after the freeze need a written justification based on requirements and a new hash, with no fix made only because the two sides agree. No exception was invented.
- M8. Revision 4 choices and partiality are retained: kicks, spawn, release and repeat, opening queue, start and blur suppression, failed reads, a dialog's other action, tap direction, high-score timing, and the remaining readings. Expected values are never borrowed from production. A defined subdomain, an allowed relation or an Undefined result is a legitimate partial claim, not automatically an owner question or a silent deterministic oracle.
- M9. A successful softDrop restart counter cannot stand for real deadlines, phase, request reissue or blocked softDrop. Real cancel errors, reporting, render and input mapping, touch pixels, legacy formats, device, P3, wake lock, browser, outer cache and QA need their own independent methods and are not to be forced into engine-model coverage.

### Other recorded unknowns (October 5, 2026)

- The property paper records that its earlier 19 static checks ran at the original P3 base, locally on Node 22 and npm 10, not the target Node 26, and that the dated baseline reported 100% metrics and mutation and a deployment (409faf4), which is not new evidence of what ships now. These statements were not independently verified at fold time.
- "Not Assessed" is a certification category, not a claim that no method exists. O5 and O8 do not replace each other, and O6 assumptions are not proof of provider correctness.
